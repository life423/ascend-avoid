# Later gameplay directions

November 4, 2026. These are proposals following the relocation, icon and HUD work.
They are not enabled mechanics or agreed balance values. The arena and bot count
stay unchanged. Keep the new injury, growth, walking, defense and camera rules
stable while assessing each idea separately.

## Black-hole size variation

Start with two recognizable sizes, **48 and 64 core radius**, rather than making
some larger than today's 64. A 48-radius core has 25% less diameter and about 44%
less lethal area. That creates a harder precision shot and slightly easier giant
routes without introducing a more obstructive maximum. Use the same warning
length for both. Choose size while dormant and lock it through warning, active
and closing phases; do not expand a live core toward a player.

The candidate validator must use that slot's actual core and visible ring. The
largest 1,470-wide body still needs connected routes and the existing corridor
clearance. World rings, collision, shot intersection and minimap markers must
agree. The current art can represent different radii; loot placement reservations
must be reviewed before any increase above the present size. Smaller holes could
prove frustrating to shoot into on phones, so assess real hits and near misses
before selecting a mix. **Recommendation: review relocation first, then try this
two-size range as its own local experiment.**

## Size-tiered weapons

Physical width should unlock the ability to complete a special build. Growth
alone should not award it. A rare core can appear at an announced, contested
location; the collector then follows the existing part guidance and claim rules.
Rivals contest the initial core or steal carried parts through the existing
mechanic. Do not quietly allow rivals to collect claimed loose pieces.

Require a visible, interruptible assembly commitment at placement. The strongest
weapon should offer a special attack pattern with a clear charge and dodge
opportunity, limited operation and the same injury/resistance rules. More injury
from tiny, unreadable hits would recreate the giant fairness problem just fixed.
Keep ownership and safe mint attack cues. Avoid additional routine satellite
population merely to make the reward feel stronger.

If the builder shrinks below the completion requirement, retain collected pieces
but pause completion. Existing claim expiry/death cleanup must release abandoned
sets. Once legitimately deployed, use its ordinary finite lifetime rather than
making it switch off every time its builder takes damage. Size thresholds,
assembly time, attack type and resource rarity still need selection. **Recommend
one special recipe first, rather than replacing the six existing weapons or
assigning every recipe a tier.**

## Deliberately aiming a fan launch

The current aperture admits bodies no wider than **54**. Fan launches already
allow limited lateral flight steering and use a separate impact profile without
the player-cannon's shooter-mass bonus. They currently injure and stun a surviving
target whenever the impact spills gems. A deliberate aim stage would make this
existing disabling interaction easier to repeat against giants.

Before adding aim control, decide a meaningful physical impact threshold for fan
injury and whether its stun must obey the same threshold. Retain modest damage
and transport against much heavier targets. Do not assume the assembled-weapon
injury rule already covers fan bodies; it does not.

A possible later interaction is a brief visible docking opportunity: steer to
aim, then use a fresh press of the existing action to launch. A bounded timeout
releases automatically, with normal post-flight recovery and no immediate repeat
entry. Input already held when entering must not unexpectedly fire. This needs a
server-authoritative state and identical bot rules, followed by desktop/joystick
checks. **Recommendation: settle the impact/stun limits before implementing the
aim stage.** No additional button or removal of the joystick is proposed.

## Evidence still needed

Recorded ordinary matches with small and giant humans, plus busy real-device
sessions, should assess reward, warning comprehension, routes and escape choices.
Geometry, input and accounting tests establish mechanical correctness; they do
not establish that these proposed mechanics are enjoyable or balanced.
